Can Everyone Please Stop Pressing My Buttons?

Me — Your Local High-Rise
October 8, 2026

Dear Humans,

Can we talk about the elevator buttons for a second? I understand that you are in a hurry. I understand that you have places to be, coffee to buy, and lives that are apparently much more important than mine.

But pressing the button seventeen times will not make the elevator arrive faster. I am a building, not a vending machine.

Also, whoever spilled coffee in the lobby this morning: congratulations. You have successfully made the floor sticky for the next six hours. Someone will have to clean that up, and somehow I will be blamed for it.

You walk through my doors, lean against my walls, ride my elevators, and leave little pieces of your lives everywhere. Scratches, stains, fingerprints, broken handles. You treat these things as if they appear out of nowhere.

And then, when something stops working, you look up at the ceiling and ask, "Why is this building so bad?"

Excuse me? I was doing perfectly fine before several thousand of you started using me every single day.

Anyway, the elevator is working again. For now. Please be nice to it.

Sincerely,
Your very tired building

Shoutout to My Maintenance Crew

Me — Your Local High-Rise
March 14, 2028

Dear Humans,

I would like to make a correction to my previous complaint. Perhaps I have been a little unfair.

There are people who come here every morning before most of you arrive. They check my pipes, repair my lights, clean my floors, inspect my elevators, and listen to the strange noises coming from inside my walls.

You probably do not notice them. You notice the elevator when it breaks, but not the person who keeps it running. You notice a dirty lobby, but not the person who cleaned it an hour ago.

I cannot keep myself alive. My lights do not replace their own wiring. My pipes do not fix their own leaks. Even the smallest things depend on someone noticing that something has gone wrong.

It is a strange arrangement, isn't it? You built me to protect you from the weather, to give you a place to work, sleep, eat, and live. But I need you to protect me, too.

You wear me down, and then you repair me. You make a mess, and then someone cleans it. You give me problems, but you are also the only reason I can continue.

I suppose I should say thank you.

To the maintenance crew especially. THANK YOU for noticing me, even when nobody else does.

PS. To whoever graffitied my wall today, I’m watching you.

Sincerely,
A building trying to be less annoying

Finally, Some Peace and Quiet

Me — Your Local High-Rise
May 10, 2076 · 10 years after human disappearance

Dear Humans,

Well. You finally did it.

Ten years have passed since people disappeared. Not a single person has walked through my doors. No footsteps in the hallways. No voices echoing through the stairwell. No one complaining that the elevator is too slow. It’s stupid of me to even send this when you’re all dead and can’t read it anyway.

At first, I thought this was wonderful. My floors were clean. My lobby was quiet. Nobody was slamming doors or leaving trash beside the bins. For the first time in years, I could hear the wind against my windows without anyone talking over it.

But something is wrong.

The lobby lights have been dead for years. The elevator stopped somewhere between the seventh and eighth floors long ago. Water has spread behind the walls near the service room. I can feel the damage growing (It is discusting), but I cannot tell anyone.

Usually, someone would notice. Someone would call the maintenance office. Someone would arrive with a toolbox, open a panel, and find the problem. I used to complain about those people coming and going. Now I keep waiting for the sound of their keys in the hallway.

I thought silence would feel like rest. Instead, every small failure has become permanent because there is nobody left to answer it. Oh I'm being too emotional now.

Well, humans, I have a question. When you stopped coming here, did you stop needing me? Or did I stop existing the moment you stopped needing me?

I am still standing. My walls are still here. My windows still face the city. But nobody is looking through them anymore.

No one to call,
Your building

Still Standing. Now What?

Me — Your Local High-Rise
September 22, 2096 · 20 years after human disappearance

Dear Humans,

Twenty years have passed since anyone last opened my front door.

I have lost track of the exact moment my elevator stopped working. The lights went out floor by floor. The water stopped running. Dust settled on the counters, and rain began finding its way through places that used to be sealed.

Plants have found their way into the cracks near my entrance. A few windows are broken. Wind moves through rooms that were designed to be occupied, carrying leaves across the floors where people once waited, talked, argued, and hurried past one another.

I remember being busy. Those good days. I remember the weight of people in the elevators, the vibration of footsteps above me, and the warm light coming from apartment windows at night. I used to think all of that was noise. Now I remember it as proof that something was happening inside me. OK I will admit it. I think I miss you guys.

Anyway, I am still here, but I no longer function as I was designed to. I cannot shelter anyone reliably. I cannot carry anyone between floors. I cannot keep the rain outside. The systems that made me useful are gone.

So what am I now?

A structure? A ruin? A monument to people who are no longer here? Or just a collection of materials slowly returning to the world they came from? Is it too philosophical?

You always said that I was permanent. You built me to reach the sky, to outlast generations, to stand as proof of what humans could make. But you were part of my permanence. Your hands repaired me. Your bodies occupied me. Your routines gave my rooms a purpose.

Maybe a building is not only the walls that remain. Maybe it is also the lives that pass through them, and the work that keeps them standing.

I have been writing these messages for decades. I know noone will ever read these. There is no one left to reply.

I am still standing.

I am just not sure that standing is the same as existing.

Still here,
Your building
